# P5 design findings and opportunities

These findings come from one shared 40-minute session with two consenting participants. See the [task list and setup](user-testing-tasks.md), [father's report](study-report-father.md), and [mother's report](study-report-mother.md). Both participants completed receiving and searching; I answered their questions, so subsequent success includes assistance.

The classifications identify areas to investigate, not proven causes. Severities are provisional: the available observations do not specify task delays, exact recovery steps, or whether participants could have continued without explanations. The shared setting also limits independent comparisons.

## 1. Starting a conversation needed clarification

**Evidence:** My father registered quickly, asked how to add friends, and sent messages quickly after my explanation of how to start a conversation.

**Possible cause:** Combining search and starting a chat may make the action hard to discover. He may also have expected a separate friend-adding step; wording and placement were not isolated as causes.

**Proposed improvement:** Test a visible **New chat** action with a short instruction to enter a friend's username. Observe whether new participants can begin without explanation.

**Level:** Physical — discoverability of the action. **Severity:** Minor, provisional; sending succeeded after clarification, but the delay and need for help remain uncertain.

## 2. The countdown's purpose needed explanation

**Evidence:** My mother asked why there was a countdown. I explained its purpose, and she completed the receiving task. Her original interpretation is unknown.

**Possible cause:** The connection between elapsed time and permission to receive new messages may not be apparent during first use.

**Proposed improvement:** Explain beside the timer that new messages become available during the window and previously received messages remain afterward. Ask participants what happens when it ends before explaining.

**Level:** Conceptual — understanding the receiving window. **Severity:** Minor, provisional; receiving succeeded with clarification, while the cost of that clarification was not measured.

## 3. The recipient's reading status was an unresolved expectation

**Evidence:** My mother asked how to know whether a recipient had read her message, and I answered. The observations do not identify a particular label she saw or misinterpreted.

**Possible cause:** She wanted information Catchup does not provide. The app distinguishes waiting from availability; it does not track reading. This is a possible expectation gap.

**Proposed improvement:** Explain that **Available** means accessible and that reading is not tracked. Investigate whether this distinction is understandable before considering read receipts, which could introduce pressure to respond.

**Level:** Conceptual — access versus reading. **Severity:** Minor, provisional; the question identifies an information need, without a reported communication failure.

## 4. Optional shape assistance could extend drawing

**Evidence:** My mother sent two drawings, said she likes drawing, described the experience as fun, and requested automatic conversion of a hand-drawn circle into a perfect circle.

**Possible cause:** The freehand pad offers limited shape assistance. Her request suggests interest in additional control, without an associated drawing failure being reported.

**Proposed improvement:** Explore an optional circle tool or shape-cleanup action while preserving freehand drawing. Test its usefulness and discoverability.

**Level:** Physical — drawing controls. **Severity:** Minor, provisional enhancement opportunity; the current pad supported her two drawings.

## Additional tradeoff: generated images

My father tentatively suggested generation to engage people less interested in drawing, including himself, but questioned whether its speed fit the time invested in handmade messages. His impression that Catchup targets drawing enthusiasts may have been influenced by my introduction. The app supports non-urgent exchanges through text and optional drawing. His suggestion raises a tradeoff to investigate, rather than establishing a need for another feature.
