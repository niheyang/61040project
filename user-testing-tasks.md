# User testing tasks and session setup

Two participants, my father and mother, actively used Catchup during one shared session lasting approximately 40 minutes. Both consented to participate and to the use of observations for coursework. I introduced Catchup as an app for friends’ non-urgent conversations, highlighting the catch-up button and drawing feature, then gave the five tasks below.

The instructions summarize the task goals; they are not verbatim quotations. The rationales explain what observing participants can reveal beyond testing the interactions myself.

| Task | Instruction | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add a friend | Add another user so you can communicate with them. | Test whether participants discover how to start a conversation. Their actions can reveal whether the combined search/start-chat control matches their expectations of adding a friend. |
| Send a message | Send that person a text message. | Observe whether composition and sending are understandable, and whether the resulting feedback helps participants interpret what happened to their message. |
| Send a drawing | Send that person a drawing. | Test whether participants discover the drawing mode and understand how to create and send visual content. Their reactions can identify what they value or want to control. |
| Receive a message | Receive a message from another user. | Investigate whether participants understand that opening a receiving window makes new messages available, and what they think the countdown controls. |
| Search for a message | Find a message in your conversations. | Test whether participants discover search and understand which conversations and content it can find. Success or hesitation can expose a mismatch between their expectations and the search behavior. |

## Facilitation and outcomes

Both participants sent text and drawings, and both completed the receiving and searching tasks. My father registered quickly and asked how to add friends. My mother asked about the countdown and how to tell whether a recipient had read a message. I answered their questions, so subsequent completion should not be interpreted as entirely unaided discovery.

The [father's report](study-report-father.md) and [mother's report](study-report-mother.md) describe each participant's observations within this same session. The [design findings](design-findings.md) distinguish observed difficulties from feature suggestions.

## Study limitations

This was one shared 40-minute session, not two independent hour-long studies. Shared participation may have influenced behavior and comments. Individual task times, exact recovery steps, and the influence of one participant on the other were not captured in the available observations. The introduction also highlighted drawing, which may have influenced participants' impressions of the app's audience.
