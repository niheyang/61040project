# User study report Father

My father tested Catchup alongside my mother in a shared 40-minute session. Both consented to participate and to the use of observations for coursework. I introduced the app as a space for friends’ non-urgent conversations, highlighting catch-up and drawing. I assigned five tasks: add a friend, send a text message, send a drawing, receive a message, and search for a message.

He registered quickly but asked how to add friends. I explained how to start a conversation. After adding the friend, he sent messages quickly and also used drawing. He completed the receiving and searching tasks. These observations suggest that the main initial difficulty was finding the entry point for communication, rather than composing messages once a conversation was established.

The combined search-and-start-chat control may not match his expectation of how to add a friend. However, the session does not establish whether its wording, placement, or another factor caused the question. A clearly labeled **New chat** action and a short username instruction would be worth testing.

He tentatively suggested text-to-image generation for people like himself who are less interested in drawing. He perceived Catchup as aimed at drawing enthusiasts, but noted that generation would be faster than drawing by hand and was unsure whether it fit the app. This presents a possible tradeoff between easier participation and the time invested in handmade messages. His audience impression may also have been influenced by my introduction, which emphasized drawing.

Overall, he communicated successfully after clarification. Because I answered his question, this outcome demonstrates successful use following assistance, rather than independent discovery. The shared setting and lack of individual task timings also limit comparisons between participants.
