# User study report Mother

My mother participated with my father in the same shared 40-minute session, with consent for coursework use of observations. I introduced Catchup as an app for friends’ non-urgent conversations and highlighted catch-up and drawing. The five assigned tasks were adding a friend, sending text, sending a drawing, receiving a message, and searching for a message.

She sent two drawings, said she likes drawing, and described the experience as fun. She also completed receiving and searching. She asked for more drawing functions, giving the example of converting a hand-drawn circle into a perfect circle. This suggests an opportunity to support more precise shapes while retaining manual creation. It does not establish that the existing tools prevented her from expressing herself.

She asked why there was a countdown, and I explained its purpose. This indicates that the timer needed clarification during first use. The question does not reveal what she initially thought the timer measured. A concise explanation beside the timer could connect the countdown to receiving new messages while making clear that earlier conversations remain accessible.

She also asked how to know whether the recipient had read her message, and I answered this question. Catchup tracks availability rather than reading. Her question identifies information she wanted that the app does not provide. Clearer status explanations could distinguish access from reading without assuming she misinterpreted a particular label.

The session supports investigating clearer explanations of the receiving window and message statuses, alongside optional drawing assistance. Because I provided clarification, later task success should not be presented as entirely unaided. These observations came from a paired session rather than an independent individual study.
