Alice finds a recipe she wants to share with Bob while he is in class. She wants to send it before she forgets, without asking him to engage immediately. After they create accounts and exchange usernames, she enters Bob's username in **Find or start a chat**, opens their conversation, and sends the link. She also uses **Draw** to add a handwritten note. With Bob's catch-up window closed, both messages show **Waiting for catch-up**. Bob can sign in and browse earlier conversations while these new messages stay hidden, with no previews or waiting counts. If he is curious, **Check for updates** briefly tells him whether anything is waiting, without identifying senders, showing content, or starting reception. Later, when he wants to hear from friends, he selects **Catch up now**. A 15-minute window opens across all his chats, and Alice's messages appear with their original timestamps. Her outgoing labels change to **Available**, telling her that Bob can access the messages, rather than that he has read them. Messages arriving during the window also become available. Bob replies and selects **Save** on the recipe so he can find it later through **Saved only**. His reply follows Alice's own catch-up window. When Bob selects **End catch-up**, or the timer expires, previously released conversations and bookmarks remain accessible; new incoming messages wait for his next window.
