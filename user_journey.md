Alice wants to send a message while Bob is busy. She wants to send before she forgets, without asking him to engage immediately. After they create accounts and exchange usernames, Alice adds Bob, opens Bob's chat and sends the message. She can send either text or doodle. Her message shows **Waiting for catch-up**, confirming that Catchup has stored it. Bob can sign in and browse earlier conversations without seeing pending messages, previews, or a waiting count. Later, when he wants to hear from friends, he selects **Catch up now**. A 15-minute window opens across all his chats, and Alice's message appears in their conversation with its original timestamp; further messages arriving during the window also become available. Bob can reply, or privately save the message to revisit. His reply follows Alice's own receiving window. When Bob selects **End catch-up**, his released history remains available, while subsequent incoming shares wait for his next window. This journey demonstrates control over when new content becomes available; it does not guarantee attention, a response, or that Bob remembers to return.
